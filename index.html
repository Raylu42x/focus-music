NOTES

01

Nothing here is speech-shaped. Irrelevant speech disrupts verbal working memory, so anything that parses as language competes with the part of you that is reading and writing. No vocals, and nothing that imitates the cadence of a voice.

02

Nothing changes state. Sound that shifts from segment to segment disrupts ordered work far more than steady sound does. So there is no beat, no groove, no repeating figure and no section boundary. Chords glide rather than cut, over a 32-second cycle, above a D that never moves.

03

Nothing modulates between 4 and 8 Hz. Speech modulates fastest around 4–5 Hz and the ear is tuned to catch it. Measured across the finished file: 0.2% of envelope energy in that band.

04

Nothing swells. A level rider holds the continuous bed flat, so loudness stays inside 0.7 dB across the middle 90% of the run. Earlier drafts wandered 13 dB and kept pulling the eye off the screen.

05

No sharp onsets. Transients trigger the orienting reflex. Bell attacks are 45 ms and sit about 7 dB under the bed; the pads take seconds to arrive.

06

Nothing can surprise you harmonically. Every pitched event is drawn from D minor pentatonic, which is consonant against all six chords, and moves by small random steps — so there is no line to follow and nothing to anticipate.

07

Dark on purpose. High-frequency energy is arousing, so the mix tilts down steadily above 1 kHz. The gap left around 1–2 kHz is deliberate: that is where speech intelligibility lives, and keeping the music out of it lets the masking noise do that job unopposed.

08

Play it quieter than feels right — roughly 45–55 dBA. It is built to sit underneath your thoughts, not beside them. If you are noticing it, it is too loud.

09

Phones. By default this plays as a plain looping audio stream with no Web Audio in the chain, so it keeps going when you lock the screen or switch apps, and it appears in your lock-screen controls. The moment you touch masking noise, warmth or the 40 Hz pulse, it has to build an audio graph — and iOS suspends audio graphs when the page goes to the background. Processing or background playback: on iPhone you get one or the other, and the output line above always says which you are on.

10

Not MP3. Served as Opus where the browser supports it and AAC everywhere else — both are markedly better than MP3 per byte, and unlike MP3 both carry proper gapless metadata, which is what lets the loop close without a seam.
